# Change Execution Rationale

This document explains the adaptive method in [change execution](change-execution.md). It owns no execution rule and cannot authorize a product or workflow decision.

## Preserve Direction While Revising Method

An agent can follow a locally reasonable route that no longer advances the requested outcome. Long context and repeated compression can hide the premise that made the route reasonable. Source intent and accepted decisions therefore need a more stable lifetime than working facts and plans. A rewritable checkpoint keeps recovery cheap without allowing a changed method to rewrite intent.

Predicting the artifact, belief, or decision an action should change exposes motion without useful progress. A failed hypothesis can be useful evidence; repeating the same method without a changed question cannot repair it. A fresh direction observation can recover a neglected alternative, but freshness alone does not prove independence or correctness.

## Keep Assurance Close To The Failure

Direct artifacts can contradict a polished completion report. Independent judgment matters when producer bias, uncertainty, or consequence makes self-review insufficient. A fixed sequence of roles for every edit spends attention without necessarily observing the relevant failure. The useful question is what error another context or check must intercept.

Tests can also preserve the wrong premise. Public-interface tests leave implementation freedom while independent authorship prevents the builder from choosing its own pass condition. Separate old/new replacement proof from a specification-first behavior-change ratchet: they answer different questions. A targeted landing gate shortens feedback; accounted full-suite batches and a separate quality assurance pass find broader failures. None of those observations can erase a known red result or relax a strict safety boundary.

## Trial A Pattern Before Making It Universal

Every mechanism costs maintenance, setup, and reader attention. An explicit necessity claim and a reasoned ablation expose whether an existing owner or a smaller method is sufficient. Experimental removal resolves uncertain claims and tests maintenance or deletion candidates; it is not a universal ritual for justified additions. Complexity numbers help locate costs but become misleading when agents optimize the numbers instead of the outcome. A two-sided inventory checks both new concepts and parallel copies because reducing one visible count can merely move duplication elsewhere.

These reasons justify the adopted rules, not a new coordination framework, runner, registry, or review quota. [The adoption decision](decisions/2026-11-04-engineering-doctrine-sync.md) records provenance and deliberate exclusions.
